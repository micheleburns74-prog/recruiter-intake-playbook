# Example hiring plan: Senior Backend Engineer, Routing

A filled-in version of the [hiring plan template](hiring-plan-template.md). The company, team and numbers are invented for illustration. It is not based on a real search, employer or candidate.

**Company:** Lanternfly Logistics, a fictional last-mile delivery platform
**Hiring manager:** Director of Engineering, Routing
**Seats:** 1

## 1. The role

The routing team owns the system that assigns drivers to deliveries in real time. The current senior engineer is carrying on-call alone, and a planned expansion into two new metro areas lands in five months. The new hire takes over the dispatch service in their first ninety days, with a mentor on the team for the first month. If the seat stays empty, the expansion launches on the old assignment logic.

## 2. What good looks like

| | Must have | Trade-off | Not wanted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Skills | Production backend in Go or Python, owns services end to end | Some experience with optimization or geospatial work, can learn on the job | Pure data science with no production ownership |
| Experience | 5+ years, at least one on-call rotation | Fewer years with clear scope and ownership | Management track only |
| Background | Marketplace, logistics or real-time systems | Strong general backend with a fast ramp | No production exposure |

Calibration from two real profiles: the manager said yes to an engineer who rebuilt a queueing system at a food delivery company, and no to a strong researcher with no on-call history. The line is production ownership, not a specific domain.

## 3. Constraints

- **Location:** remote in US time zones, with two weeks onsite in the first quarter.
- **Compensation:** posted band with a stated ceiling 10% above for an exceptional candidate, confirmed in writing.
- **Process:** recruiter screen, hiring manager conversation, technical deep dive, system design with two engineers, then a cross-functional conversation.
- **Dependencies:** headcount is approved. No internal candidate.

## 4. Sourcing plan

| Channel | Target | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outbound search | Senior and Staff backend engineers at marketplace and logistics companies | Closest match to the work |
| Target companies | Delivery, rideshare, freight tech and mapping platforms | Direct experience with dispatch and routing |
| Referrals | The routing team's former colleagues | Warmest introductions |
| Inbound | Posting plus the careers page | Fills the bottom of the pipeline |

## 5. Pipeline targets

Using the assumptions in [pipeline-math.md](pipeline-math.md):

| Stage | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Outreach sent | 70 |
| Replies | 21 |
| Recruiter screens | 12 |
| Hiring manager interviews | 5 |
| Final round | 3 |
| Offers | 1 to 2 |
| Hires | 1 |

## 6. Timeline

| Week | Milestone |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Search live, 30 messages out, first three profiles to the manager |
| 2 | Remaining outreach, first screens, manager feedback on the first batch |
| 3 to 4 | Hiring manager interviews |
| 5 to 6 | Technical and system design rounds |
| 7 | Offer |
| 8 | Close, with a start date before the expansion planning begins |

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Early signal | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Band is below what senior marketplace engineers earn | Screens decline on comp | Bring market data and use the approved ceiling |
| Manager feedback slips | A profile sits past 48 hours | One escalation, then agree a daily ten-minute slot |
| Domain requirement too narrow | Fewer than 8 screens by week 3 | Move geospatial from must-have to trade-off |
| Candidate drop-off between rounds | Silence over four days | Named next step and date after every conversation |

## 8. Cadence

A twenty-minute check-in every Tuesday: pipeline against the targets above, feedback outstanding, one decision needed. A shared tracker the manager can open any time.

## 9. What I need from you

48 hour feedback including on a no, two standing interview slots a week, the full panel named today, and the approval ceiling in writing.
