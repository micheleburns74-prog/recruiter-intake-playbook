# Pipeline math

How to turn "we need to hire one person" into outreach numbers and a timeline, so the conversation with the hiring manager has real figures in it.

The conversion rates below are illustrative starting points, not benchmarks. Replace them with your own numbers as soon as you have a few weeks of data. The method is what matters.

## The method

Start from the hire and divide backward through each stage by that stage's conversion rate.

| Stage | Conversion to next stage | Needed for 1 hire |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offer accepted | 80% | 1.25 offers |
| Final round to offer | 50% | 2.5 final rounds |
| Hiring manager interview to final | 50% | 5 hiring manager interviews |
| Recruiter screen to hiring manager interview | 40% | 12.5 recruiter screens |
| Reply to screen scheduled | 60% | 21 replies |
| Outreach to reply | 30% | about 70 outreach messages |

So one hire from a cold outbound search takes roughly seventy well-targeted messages. Two hires from the same search take about a hundred and forty, not seventy twice as fast.

## What it tells the hiring manager

- **A small pool is a risk.** If the search only turns up thirty qualified people, the plan above does not work, and you need to widen the requirements or the location before starting.
- **Slow feedback shows up in the numbers.** Every extra week between stages loses people, so the conversion rates above assume 48 hour feedback.
- **One offer is not a plan.** At an 80% acceptance rate, a single offer in play is a one in five chance of starting over.

## Timeline from the same numbers

| Stage | Typical time |
| --- | --- |
| Outreach to reply | 3 to 7 days |
| Reply to recruiter screen | 3 to 5 days |
| Screen to hiring manager interview | 5 to 7 days |
| Hiring manager interview to final round | 5 to 7 days |
| Final round to offer | 2 to 4 days |
| Offer to accepted | 3 to 7 days |

Add them up and a clean search takes six to eight weeks. Every stalled decision adds to that, which is why the intake asks for feedback turnaround and named panels.

## Tracking it

Keep one row per candidate and one column per stage. At the end of each week, count how many moved forward, divide by how many entered the stage, and compare to the assumption above. Where reality is far from the assumption is where the search needs attention.
